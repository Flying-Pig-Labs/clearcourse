METHOD / DATA / DESIGN

How this was built.

Model v4

ClearCourse turns a race brief into candidate courses. For each one it estimates which streets close, where officers and barricades go, what staffing costs and who loses road access. It runs entirely in your browser on a snapshot of public Richmond data. Every number traces to a dataset, a published rule or an assumption you can edit.

The question it answers

Given a start, a distance and any streets to require or keep open: which streets close, where do posts go, what does staffing cost, and who loses access?

It is built for the conversation between race organizers, Richmond Police, Public Works and Parks. It informs decisions; it does not approve anything.

The data

Python scripts download each layer, join street segments whose ends lie within 1.2 m into one network, keep the largest connected piece, and attach signals, traffic and facilities. A missing match means unknown, not zero. The dataset ID is a hash of the street network, so saved work and worksheets cannot attach to a different snapshot.

From brief to course

  • The search tries 64 loop shapes around the start and joins their corners along streets with an A* shortest-path search.
  • It rescales each loop up to four times toward the target distance.
  • It keeps loops within the tolerance that visit required streets and avoid keep-open streets, then offers three: balanced, lower burden and fewer turns.
  • "Favor fewer turns" adds a 150 m cost per turn during the search. "Favor smaller streets" raises the cost of arterials.
  • It answers in under a second. It is a search heuristic, not a proof of the best course.

Closures and posts

  • A post is any course junction where a public road the course does not use meets it, including freeway ramps.
  • Junctions within 22 m that share a street name merge into one post.
  • The busiest road at a post sets its category. Freeway connections default to principal arterial.
  • Alley mouths are counted. They get barricades only if you set a number.

Staffing and cost

  • Officers per post by road class: 1, 1, 2 and 3, from local street to principal arterial. These are placeholders until real staffing data calibrates them.
  • Each post is staffed from setup until the last finisher, start wave and clearance pass. Rolling closures use each post's own runner arrival times.
  • Billing follows Richmond PD's agreements: at least 4 hours per officer, and a started hour bills in full past 15 minutes. Ten or more officers fall under form PD-119SPE, requested 30 days ahead.
  • Cost = billed officer-hours × rate. The $85 rate is a placeholder.
  • Moving officers between posts is not modeled: in a 5K or 10K, no post closes before another opens.

Distance

  • Length is measured along street centerlines.
  • Certified courses use the shortest possible route, 0.30 m from the curb, which cuts every corner.
  • The estimate subtracts 2 × (centerline-to-curb distance − 0.30 m) × tan(turn angle ÷ 2) at each turn. For the default Fan 5K the estimate is 134 m (2.7%) shorter than the centerline.
  • Only a certified measurement settles the distance.

Access and emergency response

  • The screen closes the whole course at once and finds areas that lose road access.
  • It repeats the check with one-way rules to find blocks drivers can enter but not leave, or leave but not enter.
  • It times fire and EMS travel from every mapped station before and after closure and flags increases over 2 minutes.
  • It assumes emergency vehicles cannot cross the course. Real plans agree crossing points with RPD and Richmond Fire.

Imports and drawing

  • GPX and GeoJSON tracks are matched to streets with a hidden Markov model, the standard map-matching method (Newson and Krumm, 2009).
  • In testing, the app's own exports re-imported identically for 51 of 51 routes. Simulated watch tracks landed within 2% of true length for 50 of 51.
  • Drawn routes follow the shortest street path between clicks and may double back on a street.

Design choices

  • Static and private: no server, accounts or tracking. Sessions stay in this browser. A share link carries the scenario after the #, which browsers do not send to servers.
  • No third-party code runs in the page. The map is drawn directly from the street data.
  • Officers and cost lead. The burden index comes last because it is a weighting, not a staffing number.
  • Only the brief is required. Detail sits behind "How these numbers are calculated" and the model notes.

How it is tested

  • Three test suites check the model, the planning tools and every defect fixed since version 2.
  • Eighteen browser tests drive the app in Chromium, from route search to share links, at desktop and phone widths.
  • Richmond PD rules were checked against forms PD-119SPE (revised 06/09/2025) and PD-119SE (revised 07/14/2025).
  • Staffing accuracy stays unmeasured until a real event's numbers are entered in the worksheet.

What it does not know yet

  • Real staffing and invoices from past Richmond races.
  • Turn restrictions, lane widths, race-morning traffic and transit routes.
  • Elevation, emergency crossing points and detour signing.
  • Any permit decision. Those belong to RPD, Public Works and Parks.

How the numbers are calculated

Side approaches are every public road meeting a route junction that the route does not use, including freeway ramps and interchange connections. A junction with a freeway connection defaults to the principal-arterial category. Alley mouths are counted per route; they receive barricades only when you set barricades per alley mouth. Private streets and driveways are not counted.

Burden index = sum of road-class points + additional-approach points + matched-signal points + ADT points + posted-speed points. Approach points = class weight × 0.2 × additional side approaches; traffic points = ADT / 10,000 × traffic weight; speed points = max(0, posted mph − 30) / 10 × speed weight. Missing data adds no points and is separately reported as unknown. Scores with different data coverage need review. It is a relative comparison index with no 100-point ceiling. Lower means fewer weighted closure demands under this model. Road weights run 1 to 7 while default officer counts run 1 to 3, so index differences overstate staffing differences; compare officers and cost first.

Distance: route length is measured on street centerlines. Certified courses are measured on the shortest possible route, 0.30 m from the curb. The shortest-route estimate subtracts 2 × (centerline-to-curb distance − 0.30 m) × tan(turn angle ÷ 2) at each turn, capped at half the shorter adjacent segment. It is an estimate, not a certified measurement.

Officers needed = sum of officers at each post; each post keeps its officers for its whole window. The lower bound shown with it assumes officers could move between posts instantly. For 5K and 10K courses the two are equal, because no post closes before another opens. Supervision, relief and reserves are not modeled.

Billing (Richmond PD rule): each officer bills at least four hours, and a started hour bills in full once it passes 15 minutes (forms PD-119SPE ¶13 and ¶18, PD-119SE §19 and §22). Ten or more officers fall under the special-event agreement PD-119SPE: the City bills 1.5 × each officer's personal hourly rate, the request is due at least 30 calendar days ahead, and RPD's Outside Employment Coordinator sets the number of officers. Nine or fewer fall under PD-119SE, due at least 7 working days ahead. The $85 billed rate is a placeholder until invoices calibrate it. Billed officer-hours × rate = staffing cost.

Full closure: all sites close before the start and reopen after the last finisher plus clearance. Rolling estimate: sites close before the first modeled runner arrives and reopen after the last modeled runner passes. Repeat visits share a continuous duty window.

Route character: turns over 45° and repeated street segments are measured separately. Candidate searches sample 64 loop shapes with street-class penalties and up to four distance refinements, then filter by the brief’s tolerance, required streets and staffing cap. Keep-open streets block connected intersections. They do not establish a global optimum or certify distance. Vehicle one-way restrictions are ignored for these hypothetical fully closed race roads. Freeways, ramps, alleys and private streets are excluded from candidate routing. The fewer-turns preference adds a 150 m equivalent cost per turn over 45° during the search.

Imports: GPX and GeoJSON tracks are resampled every 20 m and matched to street segments within 50 m using a hidden Markov model that compares street distance with track distance. Route ends are rounded to the nearer intersection. Drawn routes follow the shortest street path between clicks and may retrace a street.

Data sources & what still needs validation

Street centerlines, functional road classes and geometry: Richmond DPW. Water and park polygons: Richmond GIS. Downloaded November 2, 2026. The bundled study area covers the Fan, downtown and nearby neighborhoods; imports outside the street network are rejected.

Planning context: Richmond Police permit guidance and FHWA street-use event guidance. These sources inform the questions to ask; they do not supply this prototype’s numerical staffing weights.

Added GIS snapshots: Richmond signalized intersections; VDOT 2025 bidirectional traffic volume; Richmond health and safety facilities. Retrieved November 2, 2026. Only in-service signal records are matched within 35 m; ADT uses aligned geometry within 25 m at two of three segment samples. Spatial joins and facility records need local verification. Co-located facility records are grouped when their street address or fire-station number agrees; the underlying inventories may classify a shared site differently. Data absence is not evidence of zero traffic or no signal.

Access screening: compares public-road connectivity before and after simultaneous closure of the selected segments and nearby parallel same-name carriageways. The undirected check finds areas cut off entirely. The directed check applies DPW one-way codes and finds intersections that can be entered but not left, or left but not entered. Fire/EMS travel time runs from every mapped fire and EMS station at posted speeds (25–40 mph by road class when unposted), assuming emergency vehicles follow one-way rules and do not cross the closed course; a change over 2 minutes is flagged. Turn restrictions, external roads outside the snapshot, capacity, live traffic and agreed emergency crossings are not modeled. Detours are distance-only probes. A connected result is not an access approval; a separated result may reflect study-boundary or GIS errors.

Calibration: actual observations stay in this browser and session exports. Errors are calculated only for matched rows with the relevant observed value. No statistical confidence or validated staffing accuracy is claimed before real observations are supplied.

Before operational use: verify the network and junction grouping, road width, driveway access, course suitability, barricade design, transit, detours, hospitals, emergency access, construction, race-day timing and the traffic-control authority’s staffing rules. No reduction in staffing is validated here.
